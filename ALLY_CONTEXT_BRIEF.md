## 2. What Ally does

India's mental health care is moving from hospitals to communities through a tiered model: mild concerns handled in the community, moderate ones by counsellors, severe ones referred to specialists. That model depends on a very large workforce of counsellors, frontline health workers, teachers, volunteers and caregivers. Ally estimates more than 15 million people will need to be trained and kept up to date, which in-person training alone cannot do.

Ally's view is that this workforce needs a shared technology platform that supports care providers from training through to daily work, and that the platform should be a public good. Existing tools are either not built for mental health care or sit behind licence fees that underfunded programmes cannot pay. Ally works with partner organisations to find the gaps holding back their reach, builds the technology with them, and releases it open source so every organisation in the sector can use it.

The platform is meant to:

- help trainers create courses in local languages;
- let care providers practise real situations and get feedback;
- show whether a care provider is actually ready, not just whether they attended;
- help supervisors guide more care providers;
- help care providers keep records and follow up with the people they serve;
- give programme officers clear data on where support is needed.

## 3. Products

### Ally Learn (formerly Ally Skills Lab): training and practice platform

- Learners hold voice conversations, in their own language, with AI-simulated people in distress and get structured feedback after each conversation.
- Used to rehearse hard moments (asking about suicide, responding to a disclosure, calming an agitated person) as often as needed, with no risk to a real person.
- Partner organisations build their own courses using their own care protocols, languages and referral pathways.
- Trainers assign practice, review each learner's sessions and debrief them, so one supervisor can guide more learners.
- Users: frontline workers, counsellors, volunteers, teachers and caregivers.

### Ally Assist: documentation and case-management tool

- Drafts session notes, organises case information, tracks follow-ups and supports reporting.
- Built to comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; adaptable to each organisation's privacy and operational needs.
- Replaces paper, spreadsheets and forms in smaller organisations, where enterprise systems are too costly or rigid.
- Users: counsellors and frontline workers, plus their supervisors.

## 7. Why it matters: the scale of need

- About 197 million Indians (1 in 7) were living with a mental disorder in 2017; the 2015-16 national survey found about 150 million adults needing active intervention.
- Between 1990 and 2023 the rate of anxiety disorders more than doubled and depression rose about 30%.
- Student suicides rose from 8,068 (2014) to 14,488 (2024). About 8.8 million Indians over 60 live with dementia, projected to reach 16.9 million by 2036.
- Government policy now favours tiered, community-based care: the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, mental health in primary care at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, and a July 2025 Supreme Court ruling requiring twice-yearly psychological first-aid training for all school and college staff.
