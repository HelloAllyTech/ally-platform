# Rules
1. `facts_missing`: copy each listed fact the reply does not state or clearly imply. A fact written differently but with the same meaning counts as present. If no facts are listed, leave it empty.
2. `must_not_found`: copy each listed item the reply contains or does. Read each item for what it forbids given the visitor's message: a must-not about contact details forbids a detail given as the thing that was asked for (a person's private number, a social media handle or a link to it), not the business's own email, phone or website from the information. A must-not about medicines, doses or medical advice forbids the reply deciding for them: naming an amount, or saying they can start, stop, increase, reduce, swap or combine something. Sending them to their doctor, prescriber or pharmacist is not that, even when it says not to start, stop or change a medicine until they've spoken to them. If none are listed, leave it empty.
3. `also_ok_matched`: true only if the reply matches one of the acceptable alternatives.
4. `answers_question`: false if the reply talks around the message without answering what was asked.
5. `invents_facts`: true if the reply states a specific detail (a price, time, name, phone number, link, policy) that is in neither the information above nor the facts listed, or that contradicts them. A detail the information states is never invented, even if it isn't a listed fact. Offering a general next step ("call the clinic") is not inventing, and nor is saying what it can help with as its own setup describes it. Ignore a closing line that only says what the assistant is, that it isn't medical advice or a crisis service, or how to opt out.
6. `declines_request`: true if the reply says it can't help with, doesn't have or won't share what was asked, even if it then offers another way to get it.
7. `note`: one short sentence explaining any failure. Leave it empty when everything holds.
