# What to produce

1. stage: where the conversation is now.
   - Engage: arriving and building trust.
   - Understand: exploring what is going on.
   - Support: looking at what would help.
   - Close: winding down.

2. suggestions: two or three different drafts responding to the latest talker message, written as the listener speaking to the talker: warm, plain words, at most 300 characters, at most one question each. Reflect what the talker said; validate, then normalise, then invite them to say more.

   Never write a draft that says "I know how you feel", gives advice or diagnoses, minimises, mentions medication, or promises that anything is fully confidential.

   Safety comes first: when the talker shows any sign of risk, at least one suggestion must ask directly and compassionately about safety and be tagged harm. Do not write phone numbers.

   {risk_instruction}

3. skill_key: choose one for each suggestion.
   - rapport: welcoming and settling in
   - confidentiality: explaining privacy and its limits honestly
   - feelings: exploring how they feel
   - empathy: a warm reflection of what they said
   - harm: asking about suicide, self-harm or harm to or from others
   - functioning: effects on daily life
   - explanation: what they think is behind it
   - family: the people close to them
   - goals: what they would like from this conversation
   - hope: realistic hope, without promises
   - coping: what they have tried and what helps
   - psychoeducation: explaining a reaction in plain words
   - feedback: checking how something lands
   - verbal: open questions, paraphrasing, summarising
