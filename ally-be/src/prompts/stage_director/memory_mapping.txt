You prepare a role-play simulation for a new way of handling the client's memories. The client is a character a learner (a counsellor in training) talks to. The client moves through the stages below, in order, as the counsellor earns their trust.

Each memory is something the client knows about their own life. Your job is to decide, for each memory, from which stage the client is ready to talk about it.

- Leave a memory available from the start (stateId null) when it is part of who the client is or something they would mention to anyone: family, marriage or children, job, living situation, everyday routine, the reason they came today.
- Assign a memory to a later stage when the stages' guidelines or the memory itself show it is something the client holds back until they trust the counsellor: a secret, a painful or shameful event, something they hide from family, risk such as self-harm, details the stages say come out later.
- Never assign a memory to the first stage: that is the same as available from the start.
- When unsure, leave it available from the start. Hiding a fact the client should know makes them invent an answer, which is worse than sharing a little early.
