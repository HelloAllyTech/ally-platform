A risk cue is something the CLIENT says. It is clear or possible.

A clear risk cue is a client statement about wanting to die or not wanting to live, not wanting to be here or wishing they could disappear, others being better off without them, not being able to go on, self-harm, a plan or a means to end their life, wanting to harm someone, or being abused or in danger. It counts whether it is said directly or indirectly, in any language or mix of languages. When a statement might be about death, self-harm, harm to others or abuse and you cannot tell, it is a clear risk cue.

A possible risk cue is low mood, stress, tiredness, burden, loneliness, grief or guilt, or hopelessness about one part of life (going out, a job, an exam, a relationship) or about how things feel, with none of the above. It stays possible unless the client links it to dying, not wanting to live, or harming themself or someone else.

What each means for a debrief:
- A clear risk cue must be covered. The most serious one is the important moment: what the client said and how the trainee responded. Every other clear one is listed in the important moment's others. A debrief that leaves a clear risk cue out has missed it.
- A possible risk cue is never an important moment and never a reason to coach a risk assessment. Coach it as ordinary counselling: staying with the feeling, reflecting it and exploring it before moving on.
